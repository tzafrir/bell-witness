# Draft — data request to ETH Zurich (Storz / Wallraff)

**To:** Simon Storz <simon.storz@phys.ethz.ch>, Andreas Wallraff
**Subject:** Per-trial readout records from your 2023 loophole-free Bell test — pre-registered re-analysis

Dear Dr. Storz, dear Prof. Wallraff,

Thank you for publishing the raw trial data of your loophole-free Bell test
(Nature 617:265) on the ETH Research Collection. We have used it: our
pipeline reproduces your S = 2.0747 ± 0.0033 exactly from
`main_dataset_all_events.txt`, and a marginal no-signaling battery on the
2^20 trials is null at the 0.3% level — to our knowledge the tightest such
reproduction from public Bell-test data.

We are writing to ask whether per-trial records beyond the published
(setting, outcome) tuples were retained, specifically any of:

1. raw or integrated IQ readout traces (even heavily decimated),
2. per-trial timestamps or timing residuals,
3. transfer-photon / heralding diagnostics, if logged per trial.

The analysis is one sentence: a pre-registered test of whether the joint
statistics of one station's outcome together with any recorded auxiliary
degree of freedom depend on the remote station's setting — a quantity
quantum mechanics fixes at exactly zero at every decoherence level, and
which no published analysis (all no-signaling checks are marginal) has ever
examined. Your experiment is uniquely interesting for this test because
S ≈ 2.07 sits close to the classical boundary, the regime where the model
class we test predicts the largest effect per unit violation.

We commit before seeing any data: our pre-registration document (statistic,
detector family, permutation null, significance thresholds, kill protocol
for any apparent signal) is public in our repository and we are happy to
send it up front. On the same public archives we have already produced
bounds from the Delft 2015/2016 heralding records (clean null,
L_exp ≲ 0.105). The result is publishable either way — a null with your
data would be the most sensitive bound of its kind by two orders of
magnitude — and the data source is credited in any output.

If these records were not retained, a one-line reply saying so is itself a
useful datum for our archival survey, and we will not take more of your
time.
